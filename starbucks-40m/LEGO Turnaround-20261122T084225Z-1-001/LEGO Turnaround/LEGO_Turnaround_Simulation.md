# LEGO | What Would You Protect in a Turnaround?

**Format** · Interactive strategy book with a physical portfolio exercise and board pitch  
**Audience** · Product leads, business learners and operational leadership teams  
**Learner role** · Turnaround lead at a fictional toy company  
**Run time** · Approximately 13–16 minutes

## What we are building

Start with a worktable piled with more proposals than the organization can staff. Learners meet leaders who each want to protect their own area, then allocate eight units of execution capacity and pitch a sequence. The case teaches how to focus a portfolio, preserve the product and customer proposition, and decide where to accept a cost to reduce complexity. The final output is a two-decision turnaround brief.

## Spreads and learner flow

### 1. Hear the competing proposals · 3 minutes

Rania says, “Our retailer is asking for regular delivery of the line that customers already buy.” Ina sets down a prototype and says, “A new theme could bring in families who pass our current shelf.” Mikael says, “Each proposal takes people away from something else. You have eight units of capacity this cycle.”

| Proposal and scene prop | Cost in capacity units | Authored evidence | What funding it changes in the scene |
| --- | ---: | --- | --- |
| **Protect core range** · building set on tray | 4 | Strong current demand and familiar components. | Keeps the core product team staffed. |
| **Improve delivery reliability** · packing board | 2 | Retailer reports inconsistent availability. | Adds a staffed availability workstream and an owner. |
| **Pilot a new theme** · prototype box | 3 | Promising idea. Customer demand is untested. | Creates a small pilot queue and follow-up decision. |
| **Build the venue concept** · scale venue mockup | 5 | Possible reach beyond retailers, with a longer lead time and a new operating model. | Creates a large workstream and a future operating commitment. |

Open all four proposals before the funded plan can be submitted. After opening the retailer note, selecting **Untested demand** reveals that this note concerns an existing range. Its right label is **Current customer demand**. The learner can correct it and proceed.

### 2. Allocate what the team can deliver · 4–5 minutes

Start with all four proposals unselected. Total used = sum of funded proposal costs, remaining = 8 − total used. A single proposal or a feasible combination can go forward. The learner has to select at least one proposal and name one unmet need before confirming. A proposed addition whose cost exceeds remaining capacity shows **Needs X more units** and leaves the previous allocation intact. Provide a receipt that lists both funded and deferred work.

| Example selection | Used and left | Trade-off to show |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Core range and delivery reliability | 6 used, 2 left | Protects a demanded product and addresses its supply issue, with capacity left unused. Both speculative ideas wait. |
| Core range and new theme pilot | 7 used, 1 left | Protects the current product and buys some evidence on a new theme. Delivery reliability needs an explicit review. |
| New theme pilot and venue concept | 8 used, 0 left | Commits to unproven or longer-term work. The current customer's availability issue remains visible. |
| Core range and venue concept | Proposed 9, exceeds capacity by 1 | The ledger retains the first selected item and says the proposed total does not fit. |

For any feasible selection, show one consequence per funded and deferred item derived from the proposal table. Record the unmet need from **Core demand**, **Delivery reliability**, **New theme evidence**, and **Venue growth**. A need is eligible when its associated proposal was deferred. On funding just one item, ask for the same unmet-need field with the eligible choices.

### 3. Explain the sequence · 3–4 minutes

Victor asks, “What happens first? What condition would make you change the plan?” For the one-item route, the proposal is automatically first and the same pitch form asks about deferred work. For multi-item routes, order them. Offer these review conditions plus a distractor **Spend the remaining units immediately**.

| First proposal | Matched review condition | What Victor says on a match |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Core range | **Review retailer reorder demand and core availability before expanding the range** | “The plan protects the existing range and gives us a point to revisit.” |
| Delivery reliability | **Recheck on-time and in-full orders before adding more complexity** | “We can see whether delivery improved before adding work.” |
| New theme pilot | **Review pilot demand before authorizing a wider launch** | “The trial has a gate before we carry the full cost.” |
| Venue concept | **Require demand and operating-economics evidence before increasing the commitment** | “You have named the condition for taking on the larger commitment.” |

Coach on the first pitch using three points **Valid use of capacity**, **Named deferred need tied to an unfunded proposal**, and **Review condition matched to the first proposal**. Show one added observation about risk, such as the current retailer issue remaining unfunded. The feedback lets learners compare plans without grading them against LEGO's historical actions.

### 4. Open the real turnaround · 3 minutes

Show the 2004 action plan before the 2005 outcome. Narrator says, “LEGO refocused on core products, worked on customer and operating performance, and reduced its exposure to some activities and assets. Its financial report describes a return to profit in 2005.” Use two horizontal or vertical bars with a common signed scale and a zero reference, comparing −1,931 with +505 DKK million on the same net-result definition. Use the report's account of multiple changes rather than attributing the financial swing to one card the learner played.

| Check | Expected answer and feedback |
| --- | --- |
| Why use a capacity ledger? | **Show which priorities the team can actually staff at the same time.** A visible cost forces trade-offs. |
| Why check retailer availability before broad expansion? | **Existing demand and delivery capacity affect whether growth can be served.** A product can be wanted and still be hard to find. |
| Is the historical profit swing evidence that one simulated choice guarantees profit? | **The annual reports document several linked changes and the overall result.** Use a defined review condition to test a proposed change. |

## Build and QA

- **Produce** one workshop, one camera move to the review table, four speaking role designs, four generic product props with visible cost labels, an eight-unit ledger, pitch card and three source-backed archive panels.
- **Persist** opened cards, first demand categorization, selected proposals, priority order, unmet-need chip, review condition, and committed pitch. On allocation edits, remove invalid order positions and any unmet-need chip that is now funded. Prompt for a new review condition if the first proposal changes. A committed pitch is a snapshot, and **Try another allocation** creates a practice attempt.
- **Access** every prop and movement from a readable list. Announce allocation changes in text and separate financial signs by labels as well as position. Include original dialogue captions and static views.
- **Acceptance** the ledger allows exactly 8 units and prevents 9. A single selection can complete. Changing the first item updates the valid review condition. Keep DKK and captioned source labels on the historical chart, including after language switching. All valid branches reach the same historical results card.
